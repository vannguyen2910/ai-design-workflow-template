## Summary
OECD evidence review on how generative AI affects learning: when it helps, when it only improves task output, and what policy should do. Main message: human-centred, pedagogy-guided use.

## Credibility note
Institutional, international, evidence synthesis, 2026. Weakness for this thesis: it focuses mainly on **schools** (teachers, students, tutoring in classrooms), not adult mentoring or professional learning. Use it for general claims about GenAI and learning, not as direct evidence for mentoring platforms.

## Evidence cards (from secondary summaries; verify and add page numbers from the report)
- Students using general-purpose GenAI often produce better work, but the gain disappears when AI is removed (for example in exams). Completing a task is not the same as learning · Source: EU Digital Skills and Jobs Platform summary (Güell Paule, updated 3 Feb 2026) · Page: __ · Use in: tech-ai
- Heavy offloading to chatbots is linked with a risk of "metacognitive laziness" (the summary's phrase; check wording in the report) · Page: __ · Use in: tech-ai
- GenAI works for learning when it is built into sound teaching strategies; unguided use does not · Page: __ · Use in: tech-ai, service design
- GenAI tutoring can adapt its questions and strategy, and the gains were larger for **less experienced** tutors (one cited result: +9 percentage points in pass rates for low-experience tutors using AI support) · Page: __ · Use in: tech-ai. The +9 figure comes from a search result snippet, so verify it
- Teacher use: 37% of lower-secondary teachers used AI in 2024; 72% worried about academic integrity · Page: __ · Use in: tech-pace (context only, school setting)
- Four recommendations: prioritise foundational skills and human relationships; fund pedagogically grounded research; build trustworthy policy (privacy, bias, transparency); ensure equitable access to devices, connectivity and training · Page: __ · Use in: tech-ai, tech-data, tech-access

## How I use it in the thesis
- Supports the argument that AI should **complement** mentors, in line with Chang 2024
- The "low-experience tutors gain most" finding is worth testing against your idea of helping first-time mentors package their expertise
