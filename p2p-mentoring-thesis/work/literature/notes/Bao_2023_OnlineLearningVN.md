## Summary
Single-author reflective essay on how COVID-19 online learning affected Vietnamese secondary and tertiary students, structured around learning motivation, student engagement and digital competence. It ends with recommendations for government, schools, teachers, parents and students.

## Credibility note
- **No primary data.** The paper states it uses existing published work only, and it combines "reflection" with a text review. The author also draws on personal observation of own students (p. 87).
- Single author, one institution, modest journal, and the conclusion admits more research and statistics are needed (p. 90).
- The study does not name a sample, a time frame or search method for its literature.
- Use it as a **pointer to underlying studies and as a source of framing**, not as evidence for numbers. Cite the underlying studies where you can.

## Evidence cards (page numbers are the journal's printed pages)
- Scope: secondary and tertiary students in Vietnam, three aspects: motivation, engagement, digital competence · p. 84 · Use in: tech-access, social factor
- Motivation fell: students struggled to keep interest, felt overloaded with tasks, and lacked academic support and feedback (citing Tran et al. 2021, Van & Thi 2021, Que 2021) · pp. 85-86 · Use in: social factor
- Engagement was hurt by disrupted home study spaces, limited interaction, and weak internet connections (citing Van & Thi 2021, Pham & Van Nghiem 2022, Dinh & Nguyen 2020) · pp. 87-88 · Use in: tech-access, social factor
- Digital competence helped: IT has been a compulsory subject since 2018, and the author cites Tran et al. (2020) to argue that city and countryside students get similar technology education · p. 88 · **conflicts with the World Bank and ADB evidence on rural gaps; compare before using** · Use in: tech-access
- Recommends **peer and cross-age mentoring** and academic support programmes to raise motivation · p. 86 and 4.2, p. 89 · Use in: social factor, thesis relevance (check wording)
- Recommends equal internet access, alternative delivery for remote students, and parent involvement · pp. 89-90 · Use in: tech-access

## How I use it in the thesis
- Shows the **social side of the access problem**: even with connectivity, motivation, interaction and home environment limit online learning in Vietnam. This supports designing for engagement, not only access
- The call for peer and cross-age mentoring is a small supportive pointer for the thesis, but cite it as an author's recommendation, not as a finding

## Notes
- Chase the underlying studies it cites: Tran et al. (2020, 2021), Van & Thi (2021), Pham & Van Nghiem (2022), Dinh & Nguyen (2020, *Social Work Education*, 39(8)). Some may be primary studies with real data
- Do not use its claim of equal digital skills between city and countryside without checking Tran et al. (2020) and comparing with World Bank 2021 and ADB 2022
