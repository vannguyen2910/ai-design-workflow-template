## Summary
Case study of Vietnam's "Internet Connection and Computers for Students" programme (launched 12 Sep 2021), which tried to close the digital gap in school education during COVID-19. It describes the challenges, the policy response, early outcomes and lessons.

## Credibility note
- The paper says on page 1 that it was written **as a basis for class discussion, not as a source of primary data**, and names are anonymised. Do not use it for statistics. Use it for context and for the structure of the problem.
- It is a school-education programme, so it is indirect evidence for adult mentoring.
- Its numbers come from secondary sources (UNICEF 2020, Statista 2021, press). Cite those originals where possible.
- The "about 1.5 million students without online access (2019–2021)" figure appears in the ADB web page abstract, but I could not find it in the PDF text. Verify before using it.
- The PDF has some date slips (for example "September to November 2019" for the programme's first months, which should be 2021). Do not copy dates without checking.

## Evidence cards (page numbers are the PDF's printed pages)
- Early 2021: about 70 million of 98 million people used the internet (cited from Statista), so about 20 million needed coverage; building infrastructure in the Central Highlands and the north is costly · p. 10 · Use in: tech-access
- Before the pandemic, 9% of children lacked IT devices or had poor conditions, and 37.9% had technical problems joining online classes (UNICEF) · p. 2 · Use in: tech-access, social
- 93% of teachers in remote areas had never used technology for teaching before the pandemic (UNICEF 2020) · p. 3 · Use in: tech-access (skills)
- Programme goals: mobile internet in 1,910 uncovered localities, 1 million devices for low-income and remote students, free platforms and 4 GB per day of data · pp. 2-3 · Use in: tech-access
- Outcomes by Oct 2021: connections installed in 283 localities and 1,910 points; over 12,550 computers and tablets, 16,000 smartphones and 74,500 other tools distributed · p. 9 · Use in: tech-access
- Challenges: uneven infrastructure, no quality control of online content, weak coordination (central versus local, civil society left out), budget and monitoring gaps · pp. 3-5, 9 · Use in: tech-access, political factor
- Lessons: target disadvantaged and remote students; quality of online learning matters as much as infrastructure; better coordination · pp. 10-11 · Use in: tech-access

## How I use it in the thesis
- Context for the Vietnam layer of `#tech-access`: connection alone does not close the gap; skills, devices and quality of content matter
- Supports a design point: a mentoring service should not assume strong bandwidth or device access for all learners, and should work on a phone
- Link to the Political factor: shows how the state responds to digital inequality
