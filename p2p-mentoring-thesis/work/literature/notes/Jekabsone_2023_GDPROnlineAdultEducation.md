## Summary
Legal analysis of how online adult education providers can comply with GDPR. It identifies five main challenges (recording of online lectures, compliance of platform providers, unauthorised access, data loss, cyberattacks) and gives recommendations. It is the closest source so far to the data-protection situation of a platform that connects adult experts and learners.

## Credibility note
- Peer-reviewed, written by a researcher with a PhD in public administration and an LLM in technology law
- Methods: doctrinal legal analysis, literature review, and a survey of 60 adult education entities in Latvia (May to June 2021) (p. 49)
- Limits: Latvia-based survey, small and conducted during COVID-19; focus is on **classroom-style recorded lectures**, not one-to-one mentoring or matching; no AI profiling; and no coverage of non-EU law such as Vietnam's
- Legal opinions are the author's reading of GDPR, not binding

## Evidence cards (page numbers are the journal's printed pages)
- Five main GDPR challenges for online adult education: recording lectures, platform provider compliance, unauthorised access, data loss, cyberattacks · Fig. 1, p. 49 · Use in: tech-data
- Consent is often not "freely given" when refusing means losing access to the session (the Zoom recording notice example); consent must be specific, informed and unambiguous, with an imbalance of power in the relationship · pp. 50-51 · Use in: tech-data, ethics plan (0.5), Legal factor
- Contractual obligation (Art. 6.1(b)) or legitimate interests can serve as a lawful basis for recording, if learners are told before they sign · pp. 51, 53 · Use in: Legal factor
- Platform providers (Teams, Zoom, Google Meet) act as **processors**; they may be **joint controllers** if they use data for their own purposes (citing the Fashion ID CJEU case), so roles and duties must be set in contracts · pp. 54-55 · Use in: tech-data, Legal factor, service blueprint
- Platforms collect names and email addresses for accounts, plus cookies and online identifiers · p. 54 · Use in: tech-data
- Unauthorised access: need authentication per user and clear security duties in the learner agreement · p. 55 · Use in: tech-data
- Cyberattacks: the human factor is the weakest link; minimise personal data on the platform, audit vendors, train staff · p. 56 · Use in: tech-data
- Conclusion: three lawful routes for recording (consent, contract, legitimate interests), with open practical issues such as withdrawal after recording · p. 58 · Use in: Legal factor
