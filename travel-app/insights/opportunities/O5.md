## Decision (proposed)
Should visitors be able to browse every mode without an account, and be asked to sign up only when they press Save or Connect? The team's current choice is browsing without an account (D2).

## Insight
Most people like the idea (11 of 12) but stop before they try it, mainly because of price, then because they do not want to share their location, and in a few cases because none of their friends use it. People want to see what the app gives them before they pay or sign up. This matters for the decision above: a gate that comes too early loses people who would have stayed.

## Sources

| Source ID | Type | Verbatim quote | What it supports |
|---|---|---|---|
| SV-01 | Survey, free text | "Sounds great but I'm not paying a subscription for an app I haven't tried." | Price before trying |
| SV-12 | Survey, free text | "It wants my location all the time? No thanks." | Location privacy |
| R03 | Store review (competitor app) | "Forced me to create an account before showing anything. Deleted." | Early sign-up gate |
| F06 | Forum | "Nobody in my group uses it, so what's the point of joining?" | Friends not on the app |
| I01-07 | Interview P1 | "I'd switch if it saves me from juggling ten apps. But show me that first, then ask for an email." | Value shown before sign-up |

Strength: medium. 5 notes from 4 kinds of source, and the survey count is the clearest (7 of 13 name price). The claim that an early sign-up gate makes people leave rests on one review (R03) and one interview (I01-07).

## What these sources do not tell us
- How many people leave at the sign-up gate: nobody has measured it, and the prototype signs people in instantly.
- What price people would accept. Nobody was asked for a number.
- The sample is 13 answers and one interview. The review comes from someone who already deleted the app.

## Challenge
1. **Where we say more than the data:** "price is the number 1 barrier". It is 7 of 13, a small majority, and only SV-01 says it in words.
2. **Source that can mislead:** R03. It is about a competitor and about someone who left, so it shows what annoys people, not what the Travel Buddy gate will do.
3. **Other explanations:**
   - People are tired of subscriptions in general, not of this app: SV-01 "a subscription for an app I haven't tried".
   - People do not trust apps with their location: SV-12 does not mention price at all.
   - People need friends on the app, whatever it costs: F06 "so what's the point of joining?"
4. **Data that disagrees or was left out:** 11 of 12 find the idea appealing, so interest is not the problem. That is in the survey but not in the table above.
5. **New data that would make the insight wrong:** visitors who browse freely, press Save, and still leave at sign-up. Then the gate position is not the issue.

**What changed our mind:** keep the insight. Test the sign-up gate with a real build, because the current prototype cannot.

**Dissent kept:** the three other explanations. Privacy in particular pulls against the journal's automatic recording.

## Assumption map
- Price is the main barrier: Confirmed on a small sample. 7 of 13 in the survey; SV-01 and R03 agree.
- Friends already on the app matter: Unknown. Only 2 of 13 and one forum note (F06).
- Location privacy is rising as a barrier: Unconfirmed. 4 of 13, up from fewer, but it is a small change.

## Why it ranks here
This is the precondition for every other need to be reached. It needs a free-first model and value that can be seen before asking for sign-up.
