## Decision (proposed)
Should the trip journal record itself (location and time) and stay private until the traveller shares it with chosen people? The team's current choice is a self-recording, private-by-default journal (D7).

## Insight
Travellers want a record of the trip but stop writing one within a day or two. They take photos, then lose the name of the place and what made it good, and they tell themselves they will remember later and do not. A journal that fills itself removes the effort, but people only accept it if they can edit and delete what it recorded. This matters for the decision above.

## Sources

| Source ID | Type | Verbatim quote | What it supports |
|---|---|---|---|
| SV-05 | Survey, free text | "I always plan to write something at night and I'm asleep by 9 after walking all day." | Effort stops the habit |
| SV-11 | Survey, free text | "Only if it does it for me. I won't type anything on holiday." | Wants it automatic |
| I01-04 | Interview P1 | "I have 400 photos and no idea what that little cafe was called. I thought I'd remember." | Photos without names or details |
| I01-05 | Interview P1 | "Automatic is fine, as long as I can delete the embarrassing stuff and fix mistakes." | Accepts automatic with edit and delete |
| F04 | Forum | "Tried three travel journal apps. Quit all of them after day 3." | Quitting early |

Strength: medium. 5 notes from 3 kinds of source (survey, interview, forum). The need to remember is in several notes; the claim that people would use an automatic journal after the trip is in none of them.

## What these sources do not tell us
- Whether people open the journal after the trip. All five notes are about recording, none is about reading it back.
- How many people want this: 4 of 13 record nothing, and that is the only count.
- Whether people mean it when they say they want it. Survey answers and one interview show a wish, not behaviour.

## Challenge
1. **Where we say more than the data:** "a real need". The notes show that recording is hard, not that people value the result enough to use it. Nobody says they went back to an old journal.
2. **Source that can mislead:** F04. A forum poster who tried three apps is a keen journaller, not the typical traveller, so this quote overstates how common the habit is.
3. **Other explanations:**
   - Photos are already the journal: I01-04 "I have 400 photos".
   - The need is a better photo album, not a journal: SV-11 asks for something that does it for them, not for text.
   - People do not want a trace of the trip at all: 4 of 13 record nothing.
4. **Data that disagrees or was left out:** 4 of 13 record nothing and do not say they want to. They are in the survey but not in the table above.
5. **New data that would make the insight wrong:** people who get an automatic journal and still do not open it after the trip. Then the problem was never the effort.

**What changed our mind:** keep the insight but at low confidence, and run the diary study before building this as a core feature.

**Dissent kept:** the three other explanations. The diary study is the only way to tell them apart.

## Assumption map
- Travellers abandon high-effort journaling: Confirmed on a small sample. SV-05, F04 and I01-04 agree.
- Travellers want to share their journal publicly: Unknown. I01-05 mentions editing and deleting, which suggests the opposite, but it is one note.
- Automatic location recording is acceptable: Unconfirmed. 8 of 13 chose it, but location privacy is also a barrier to trying the app.

## Why it ranks here
A real need but the effort threshold is too high. Automatic recording and presentation have to come together. It is unclear whether this is behaviour or a wish, so research on journaling is needed before building it as a core feature.
