## Decision (proposed)
How should a tip show that it can be trusted: by the contributor's identity and travel style, or by a popularity badge like the big review sites? The team's current choice is a crowd signal (green, amber, red) instead of a badge (D3).

## Insight
Travellers do not lack information, they lack a way to tell which information is honest. They trust a person they can place (a local, a friend) more than a score from strangers, and they cross-check two or three sources before they believe a place is good. A popularity badge works against this, because a high score is exactly what drew them to the places that disappointed them. This matters for the decision above.

## Sources

| Source ID | Type | Verbatim quote | What it supports |
|---|---|---|---|
| SV-03 | Survey, free text | "Everything near the main square has 4.5 stars and tastes the same. I can't tell who is paid to be there." | Stars do not separate honest from commercial |
| SV-09 | Survey, free text | "I only believe a tip if I can see who wrote it and why they like the place." | Trust comes from knowing the contributor |
| R01 | Store review (competitor app) | "Top-rated restaurant, 4.8, and it was a tourist trap with a laminated menu." | A high rating led to a disappointing place |
| F01 | Forum | "I screenshot three different sites and only go if at least two agree." | Cross-checking as a trust filter |
| I01-02 | Interview P1 | "A single source is just an opinion. When a local friend and a blog say the same thing, then I go." | Cross-checking, and the weight given to local people |

Strength: strong. 5 notes from 5 sources, 4 kinds of source (survey, review, forum, interview), and the survey numbers (9 of 13 name it the top frustration; 10 of 13 were once disappointed by a highly rated place) point the same way. The idea that it is commercialisation that breaks trust rests on one note (SV-03).

## What these sources do not tell us
- How many people would trust a tip from a contributor they have never heard of: every quote is about a local or a friend they already know.
- Whether people notice a crowd signal at all, or read it as a quality score: nobody in the notes has seen one.
- The sample is 13 survey answers and one interview, skewed to young people in Asia. The review and the forum note come from people who were already unhappy.

## Challenge
1. **Where we say more than the data:** the phrase "non-commercial". Only SV-03 links the problem to money. R01 and F01 complain about a bad match, not about who paid for it.
2. **Source that can mislead:** R01. The quote is exact, but it is about a competitor's rating, not about local tips. It supports "stars can fail", not "tips from people are better".
3. **Other explanations:**
   - The problem is freshness, not trust: R01 "the place had changed owner since the last good review".
   - The problem is fit to the traveller, not honesty: F02 "great for families, awful for us, nobody says who it is for".
   - People distrust a single source on principle, whoever it is: I01-02 "a single source is just an opinion".
4. **Data that disagrees or was left out:** the survey rates stars from big sites at 3.38/5, not zero, so people still use them to shortlist. F03 ("I start with the stars, then check") is not yet in the table above.
5. **New data that would make the insight wrong:** users who see the identity of the contributor and still go to the places with the highest crowd. Then identity is not what they use to judge.

**What changed our mind:** keep the insight, but say "who wrote it" rather than "non-commercial", and add F02 and F03 for review.

**Dissent kept:** the three other explanations above. We have no data to rule any of them out.

## Assumption map
- Travellers distrust commercial reviews: Confirmed on a small sample. Survey 3.31/5 for strangers' reviews; P1 does not trust a single source.
- Travellers want places where locals actually go: Confirmed on a small sample. 4.46/5 for local tips; P1 asks for it by name.
- A crowd signal is understood without explanation: Unknown. No one in the notes has seen one.

## Why it ranks here
Trust in the source is the core problem and the most frequent frustration (9 of 13). The design challenge is to keep quality without letting the content become commercialised over time, which is exactly what made people lose trust in the big review sites.
